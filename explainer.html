Overview
What Is the Finance Competency Framework?
A clear, consistent picture of what great looks like, for every Finance professional, in every Finance function.
In plain language

The Finance Capability and Competency Framework describes what great performance looks like across roles in the Finance function at Fortescue. It covers the skills, behaviours and standards that matter, and shows how they evolve as you grow in your career.

It is structured around a set of competencies, broad capability areas, each with clear descriptions of what 'good' looks like at different levels of proficiency. Every Finance function has its own version, written specifically for the work that function does.

Most important thing to know
This is a development tool, not a promotion matrix. It is here to help you understand where you are today, what great looks like at the next level, and how to build a focused plan to get there. It does not determine your title, band or pay.
The seven Finance functions covered
Corporate Finance
Strategic financial oversight, capital allocation and corporate governance
Finance & Accounting
Financial reporting, controls, reconciliations and compliance
Finance Business Partnering
Partnering with the business to provide financial insight and decision support
Group Risk & Insurance (GRAI)
Enterprise risk management, insurance and assurance across the group
Planning & Analysis
Financial planning, forecasting, modelling and performance analysis
Tax
Tax compliance, structuring and advisory across all jurisdictions
Treasury
Cash management, debt, hedging and liquidity management
Same competencies, different detail
All seven functions share the same set of competencies. What differs is the observable statements, key behaviours and contributing skills, which are written specifically for the work each function does. Same structure. Tailored content.
Purpose
Why Was It Developed?
Finance plays a critical role across Fortescue's operations, globally. To do that consistently and at pace, everyone needs the same foundation.
The problem it solves

Fortescue operates at pace, in complex environments, and increasingly on a global stage. Finance is at the centre of that, managing everything from daily controls and reporting through to strategic decisions, group risk, tax, treasury and business partnering across the organisation.

Without a shared picture of what great looks like, it is hard to have meaningful development conversations, hard to know where to focus your growth, and hard to build a Finance function that performs consistently at every level.

Without it
Development conversations are vague. Growth is unclear. People are unsure what they're being measured against.
With it
Clear expectations. Specific feedback. A focused development plan grounded in what the business actually needs.
Where it came from

This framework was developed in response to the People Experience Survey and a drive to strengthen the capability and performance of the Finance function. Initiated from the top down, with direction from the CFO, it was built to support Finance in meeting the evolving needs of the business, improving proficiency across the function, and giving every person the tools to understand where they are and how to grow.

The bigger picture
Individually strong Finance professionals, working consistently well, directly enables Fortescue to deliver on its broader strategy, its Light on the Hill. The competency framework is how Finance connects individual growth to business outcomes.
Development Process
How Was It Built?
Built with Finance, not for Finance. The process was iterative, collaborative and grounded in what the function actually does.
1
Started with Fortescue's strategy and the CFO's priorities
The starting point was Fortescue's broader organisational development approach and the strategic priorities the CFO had set for the Finance function. The framework had to connect to where Fortescue is going, not just where it has been.
2
Identified the core competencies
The team worked through the capability areas that matter most across all Finance roles, the things that every Finance professional needs, regardless of which team they sit in. These competencies are consistent across all seven functions.
3
Iterated and refined, multiple times
The content went through various iterations to ensure the wording was accurate, clear and appropriately calibrated. This was not a single draft, it was reviewed, challenged and refined to make sure it was right.
4
Finance leaders took ownership
Finance leaders reviewed the content for their specific function and took responsibility for the language and expectations. This is their framework, built to reflect the real work their teams do, and owned by the people who lead those teams.
5
Validated for consistency across functions
The proficiency levels and role mappings were reviewed across all seven functions to ensure they were consistent and fair, so someone at a similar level in one function is benchmarked comparably to someone at the same level in another.
On Competencies vs Skills
The competencies themselves are designed to remain stable, they represent enduring capability areas that Finance will always need. The contributing skills within each competency can and will evolve over time, as technology, tools and the business change. The framework is built to flex where it needs to, without losing its foundations.
Proficiency Scale
The Four Proficiency Levels
The same four levels used in Fortescue's Leadership Framework, a consistent language across the organisation for describing capability and performance.
Level 1
Developing
Understands basic concepts. Requires supervision. Focused on accurate task execution. You are learning the role and building your foundation.
Level 2
Practising
Performs the role independently. Applies knowledge to resolve common problems without guidance. You can be relied on to deliver.
Level 3
Skilled
Leads functional decisions. Coaches others. Applies insights to improve outcomes. You are shaping how the team works, not just working in it.
Level 4
Advanced
Shapes policy and strategy. Drives cross-functional or international collaboration. Advises executives. You are leading Finance at the highest level.
Aligned to the Leadership Framework
These four proficiency levels are the same ones used across Fortescue's Leadership Framework. That means the language of capability is consistent, whether you are talking about Finance capability, leadership capability, or anything else across the organisation.
What levels mean for different roles

The framework maps each role level (Advisor/Accountant, Senior Advisor, Principal, Manager, Senior Manager, GM) to an expected proficiency level for each competency. This gives you a clear picture of what is expected at your level, and what you would need to demonstrate to grow into the next one.

Framework Anatomy
How to Read the Framework
Each function's framework is built the same way. Here is what each component means.
ComponentWhat it isWhat it tells you
CompetencyThe broad capability area, e.g. "Technical Ability & Controls Excellence"The high-level skill domain relevant to every Finance professional, the same across all functions
Observable StatementA plain-language description of what this looks like in practice at each proficiency levelThe standard you are assessed against, different for each function, written for the same level
Key BehavioursThe specific actions and conduct expected at each proficiency levelA practical checklist of how you show up, the 'how' behind the observable statement
Contributing SkillsThe technical and functional skills that enable those behavioursWhat you need to build or strengthen, these can evolve as the business changes
Proficiency LevelLevel 1 (Developing) through Level 4 (Advanced)Where you currently sit, and the clear path to where you could go next
Reading across levels
For each competency, read horizontally across the proficiency levels to see how expectations grow. Level 1 describes someone who is developing in this area. By Level 4, the expectation is that the person is shaping strategy and driving outcomes at the most senior level.
Reading across functions
You cannot simply track across functions and assume the same expectations apply. A Principal in Finance & Accounting and a Principal in Tax will be at a broadly similar proficiency level, but the specific behaviours and skills are different, reflecting the real work of each function. This is intentional. It means moving between functions is genuinely supported by the framework, because you can see exactly what you would need to build to transition successfully.
Practical Guide
How to Use It
The framework is a practical, ongoing reference, not a one-off exercise.
For employees
1
Read your function's framework
Start with the observable statements for your current role level across each competency.
2
Honestly self-assess
Where do you feel confident? Where are the gaps? Use the key behaviours as a practical checklist.
3
Look at the next level
Understand what you would need to demonstrate to grow into the level above yours.
4
Have the conversation
Come to your development conversation with your manager prepared, with a view of your strengths and focus areas.
For managers
1
Use specific language
The framework gives you concrete language for development conversations, not vague feedback.
2
Support honest self-assessment
Help your team members reflect accurately and set meaningful, specific development goals.
3
Spot team-wide patterns
Where does the team collectively need to grow? The framework helps you see that across the whole group.
4
A shared standard, not a judgement
Reference it in performance conversations as an objective, shared benchmark, not a personal assessment.
What the framework is not
Myth
"If I'm assessed at a high level, I'll automatically get promoted or a pay rise."
Reality
The framework is a development tool. It does not determine your role, title or remuneration, it helps you grow your capability.
Myth
"I'll read it once and I'm done."
Reality
Return to it regularly. Use it before development conversations, when you change roles, or when you want to explore moving to a different function.
